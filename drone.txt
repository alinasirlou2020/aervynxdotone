# 3. THE CORE ECONOMIC ENGINE

Build the economic engine around a rigorous, transparent, period-based financial calculation.

The primary calculation must be implemented in JavaScript, not hardcoded into HTML.

Use a centralized financial state and pure calculation functions.

## 3.1 Revenue Engine

Model the following possible revenue sources:

1. Completed cargo delivery missions.
2. Contracted logistics services.
3. Recurring commercial delivery agreements.
4. Enterprise fleet utilization contracts.
5. Other approved operational revenue.

Each revenue record should conceptually include:

- Revenue ID.
- Fleet ID.
- Drone ID or applicable fleet allocation.
- Mission ID.
- Revenue source.
- Gross amount.
- Currency.
- Collection status.
- Settlement timestamp.
- External reference.
- Verification status.

Only collected and eligible revenue should enter the finalized revenue calculation.

A completed mission is not automatically equivalent to collected revenue.

Distinguish:

- Estimated Revenue.
- Invoiced Revenue.
- Collected Revenue.
- Reversed Revenue.
- Finalized Revenue.

Implement the following:

\[
GrossRevenue=\sum_{i=1}^{n}CollectedRevenue_i
\]

\[
NetRevenue=GrossRevenue-Refunds-Chargebacks
\]

Never distribute revenue that has not actually been collected and finalized.

## 3.2 Operational Expense Engine

Create an expense model with individually configurable categories:

- Flight and mission execution.
- Battery charging and replacement.
- Maintenance and repair.
- Insurance.
- Logistics and ground handling.
- Communications and connectivity.
- Software and infrastructure.
- Regulatory and operational fees.
- Personnel and operational support.
- Allocated administrative overhead.
- Other approved expenses.

Each expense must be identifiable, attributable to an asset or fleet, and associated with a reporting period.

Formula:

\[
TotalOperatingCost=\sum_{j=1}^{m}EligibleExpense_j
\]

Do not silently subtract the same expense twice.

Display the expense breakdown in an interactive financial panel.

## 3.3 Reserve Engine

Create configurable reserves for:

- Preventive maintenance.
- Emergency repairs.
- Battery replacement.
- Insurance obligations.
- Operational continuity.
- Other contractually approved reserves.

Use:

\[
ReserveContribution=ConfiguredReserveAmount
\]

The amount may be a fixed amount, a percentage of eligible revenue, or another explicitly configured policy.

Clearly distinguish:
- Reserve contributions.
- Actual reserve expenditures.
- Remaining reserve balance.

Do not treat reserve contributions as investor profit.

Do not treat the reserve as operator revenue.

Do not double-count reserve expenditure if the reserve has already been funded in a previous period.

## 3.4 Net Distributable Profit

Use this primary model:

\[
NetRevenue=GrossCollectedRevenue-Refunds-Chargebacks
\]

\[
EligibleOperatingCosts=\sum EligibleExpenses
\]

\[
DistributableProfit=
\max(0,\ NetRevenue-EligibleOperatingCosts-ReserveContribution-CarryForwardLoss)
\]

Where:

- GrossCollectedRevenue is actually collected revenue.
- Refunds and chargebacks reduce eligible revenue.
- EligibleOperatingCosts are validated operating expenses.
- ReserveContribution is the required reserve allocation for the period.
- CarryForwardLoss is a previously recorded loss that must be addressed before profit distribution, if the contractual waterfall requires it.

The carry-forward treatment must be configurable and clearly documented.

Do not distribute negative profit.

If the period produces a loss:
- Set the current distributable profit to zero.
- Record the loss.
- Show its effect on future distributions.
- Do not create a negative investor claim.
- Do not fabricate a positive yield.

## 3.5 50/50 Allocation Engine

This is the central product rule.

\[
InvestorPool=DistributableProfit\times0.50
\]

\[
OperatorAllocation=DistributableProfit\times0.50
\]

The allocation must be displayed as two clearly separated financial flows.

The investor pool is the entire pool allocated to eligible token holders.

The operator allocation belongs to the operator under the assumed contractual model.

Do not create a 40/35/25 investor allocation.

Do not confuse reserve allocation with the 50/50 profit split.

Do not calculate the 50% allocation from gross revenue.

## 3.6 Token Holder Reward Engine

Let:

- \(S_e\) = Total eligible token supply for the reporting period.
- \(B_i\) = Eligible token balance of investor \(i\).
- \(I_p\) = Investor distribution pool.

Then:

\[
InvestorReward_i=I_p\times\frac{B_i}{S_e}
\]

For an individual token:

\[
RewardPerToken=\frac{InvestorPool}{EligibleTokenSupply}
\]

Example:

- Investor pool: $25,000.
- Eligible token supply: 10,000 tokens.
- Reward per token: $2.50.

Investor examples:

| Eligible tokens | Distribution |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | $2.50 |
| 10 | $25 |
| 50 | $125 |
| 100 | $250 |
| 500 | $1,250 |
| 1,000 | $2,500 |

These are example calculations, not guaranteed returns.

Use correct decimal arithmetic. For financial calculations, avoid relying on JavaScript floating-point arithmetic for token accounting.

Prefer integer base units or a decimal arithmetic library if one is already available. If no library exists, implement careful fixed-point arithmetic with explicit precision and rounding rules.

Document rounding dust and where it remains.
